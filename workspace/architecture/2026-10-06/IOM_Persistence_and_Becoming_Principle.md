# IOM Persistence-and-Becoming Principle

**Status:** public architecture draft  
**Date of synthesis:** 2026-10-06  
**Role:** viability law for recursive persistence and transformation  
**Publication status:** published on an additive workspace branch; does not supersede `main`

## 1. Core formulation

> **Enough structure to persist; enough variation to become.**

The relationship between coherence and entropy in IOM is not exact equilibrium, equal weighting, or the elimination of one by the other. It is a variable, context-dependent relation in which coherence supplies sufficient continuity for a system to remain organized while entropy supplies sufficient variation for it to transform.

Maximum coherence is not the universal goal. Complete coherence can become rigidity: a state in which difference is suppressed, alternatives disappear, and recursion reproduces only what already exists. Complete elimination of entropy or variation would therefore be detrimental rather than ideal.

Likewise, maximum entropy is not generative freedom. Variation without sufficient coherence becomes fragmentation or dissolution because no organized identity persists long enough to incorporate the change.

Viable recursion requires both persistence and becoming.

## 2. Complementary functions

| Quantity | Necessary function | Failure at insufficiency | Failure at excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coherence | Organization, continuity, identity, memory, relational stability | Fragmentation; inability to preserve a usable pattern | Rigidity, closure, repetitive reproduction, inability to become |
| Entropy | Differentiation, openness, novelty, movement, reorganization | Stagnation; inability to generate a genuinely new state | Dissolution; inability to integrate variation into a persistent structure |

Coherence and entropy are therefore not simple enemies. Each becomes destructive when it eliminates the contribution of the other.

## 3. Viability is a region, not a point

The relation should not be represented by a universal equality such as $C=E$, nor by a fixed ideal ratio. It is better represented as a moving viability region:

$$
\boxed{
(C_t,E_t)\in\Omega_t
}
$$

where:

- $C_t$ is effective coherence at recursive state $t$;
- $E_t$ is the operative expression of entropy in the domain being modeled; and
- $\Omega_t$ is the context-dependent region in which both persistence and becoming remain possible.

In general,

$$
\Omega_t\neq\Omega_{t+1}.
$$

The viable relation may change with scale, dimensional level, accumulated history, surrounding relationships, current geometry, unresolved information, and the transformation presently required.

## 4. Dual viability conditions

Let

$$
P(C,E;\chi)
$$

measure the system's capacity to persist, and let

$$
B(C,E;\chi)
$$

measure its capacity to become, where $\chi$ contains relevant context, geometry, history, and scale.

Then the viable region may be defined provisionally as

$$
\boxed{
\Omega(\chi)
=
\left\{
(C,E):
P(C,E;\chi)\ge P_{\min},
\quad
B(C,E;\chi)\ge B_{\min}
\right\}.
}
$$

A state is viable only if it can remain organized and remain capable of transformation. These equations are AI formalizations of the author's conceptual relationship.

## 5. Dynamic asymmetry

The viable relation need not be symmetrical. A system close to dissolution may require a strong coherence-producing operation and relatively little additional variation. A rigid system may require a strong differentiating operation and very little additional consolidation. The magnitude and direction of correction depend on the system's actual state.

This means that balance in IOM is:

- dynamic rather than static;
- bounded rather than maximized;
- asymmetric rather than necessarily equal;
- contextual rather than universal;
- historical rather than memoryless; and
- generative rather than merely stabilizing.

## 6. Relationship to the inverse operation

The inverse operation does not drive every system toward maximum coherence or toward one ideal state. It responds to the direction in which recursive viability is being lost.

$$
\boxed{
u_t^*
=
\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal U_t}
\left[
d\!\left(F(X_t,u),\Omega_{t+1}\right)
+
\lambda\,\operatorname{cost}(u)
\right]
}
$$

The selected operation $u_t^*$ moves the predicted next state toward the relevant viability region while respecting the cost or constraint of the transformation.

This creates several distinct corrective regimes:

| State | Needed direction |
|---|---|
| Excessive coherence / rigidity | Differentiation, openness, variation, release of constraint |
| Excessive entropy / dissolution | Integration, structure, constraint, relational consolidation |
| Persistence without becoming | Generative disruption or expansion of accessible alternatives |
| Variation without persistence | Formation of durable relationships and memory |
| Viable recursion | Continued transformation without compulsory correction toward a fixed point |

## 7. Relationship to the spatial architecture

The multilevel inverse relation explains how transformation moves through geometry, winding, dimensional nesting, projection, and return. The persistence-and-becoming principle explains how an organized identity can survive that transformation without becoming permanently fixed. The tilted tesseract gives this asymmetry its spatial expression and forward-driving effect: it keeps recursive movement from resolving into perfect repetition. The author's “tilt” and “offset” describe the same perceived non-alignment; the exact geometric decomposition and its mathematical connection to coherence and entropy remain to be defined.

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Multilevel inversion}
&\rightarrow \text{transformation and recursive return},\\
\text{persistence-and-becoming viability}
&\rightarrow \text{continuity through transformation}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

The principle is universal within the architecture, while its measurable variables and implementation can differ across scale-specific sub-branches. It applies at multiple scales:

- an eigenstate must preserve identity while integrating difference;
- a recursive loop must maintain enough structure to carry information while remaining transformable;
- a collective boundary must retain shared organization without erasing individual variation;
- a dimensional domain must preserve relational continuity while participating in nested transformations; and
- the toroidal web must remain coherent enough to propagate structure while each return alters the conditions of renewed projection.

## 8. Relation to recursive competency

Recursive competency is not simply the accumulation of information. It is the increasing capacity to remain coherent while admitting, discerning, integrating, and being changed by relevant variation.

A recursively competent system can:

1. preserve the relationships necessary for continuity;
2. admit difference without automatically treating it as destruction;
3. distinguish generative variation from disorganizing variation;
4. integrate what is geometrically relevant without homogenizing it;
5. retain unresolved information without allowing it to dominate the whole;
6. change its future processing conditions based on returned consequences; and
7. expand into a new state without erasing the history from which it emerged.

## 9. Entropy as the core term

Entropy remains the author's core term. “Entropy-like variation” should not replace it in the conceptual architecture because doing so weakens the asserted relationship and can sound like avoidance of the claim. The precise formulation is instead:

> **Entropy is the universal architectural category; variation is the function of entropy emphasized in this relationship.**

Physical, informational, cognitive, biological, and cosmological implementations may require different measurable definitions. Those differences belong in scale-specific sub-branches, where each branch must state how its entropy variable is calculated and how it maps to the universal principle. This preserves the core claim without silently treating every domain-specific measurement as the same physical quantity.
