# Inside-Out Model

**Purpose:** cohesive whole-model summary incorporating the recovered spatial-generative architecture, local processing pathway, multilevel inverse relation, and persistence-and-becoming principle  
**Publication status:** not approved for public release

## Integrated summary

The Inside-Out Model, historically called the Inside-Out Projection Model or IOPM, describes reality as an inside-out, recursively generated informational structure. Projection unfolds outward from the quantum and Planck-scale domain through the information and consciousness that constitute the toroidal web, from which extension, differentiation, distance, time, matter, and observable form emerge through projection. Information and consciousness are treated as fundamental to the architecture, while ordinary physical reality is understood as the stabilized local rendering of deeper relational processes. The apparent smallness of the quantum and Planck-scale domain is understood in relation to the way reality is measured linearly from our position within it rather than as an absolute indication of its position within the deeper architecture.

The universe in IOM is not fundamentally a collection of separate objects occupying an external container. It is an infinitely recursive, dimensionally nested toroidal web whose underlying geometric structure is organized through the overlapping stellated octahedron and tilted tesseract. The tesseract’s tilt provides the asymmetric effect that allows for infinite movement and growth. Toroidal domains are embedded within, projected from, and relationally linked to other toroidal domains across dimensional depth and scale. Their orientations are not independent. They participate in inverse and complementary rotational relationships that recur as handedness, nested counter-rotation, paired winding, outward and inward information flow, and inversion under dimensional observation.

The composite geometry, recursive toroidal web, and local information-processing pathway describe different scales of the same architecture. The global spatial map establishes where and how relationships can exist. The local pathway describes how information traverses a particular dimensional and eigenstate-specific route. That pathway is:

$$
\text{Higher-Dimensional Geometry}
\rightarrow
\text{Dimensional Boundary}
\rightarrow
L_1
\rightarrow
T_C
\rightarrow
L_2
\rightarrow
\Psi/\mathcal A
\rightarrow
\text{Observable Projection}.
$$

The dimensional boundary contains the governing conditions of the relevant system or dimension and the collective information contributed by the eigenstates within it. The boundary itself cannot be changed by individual eigenstates. Individuals can change their own lens, and the recursions of eigenstates can inform the contents of the boundary, but no change to the boundary occurs until the aggregate-of-the-whole condition at the relevant scale is met.

Lens 1, $L_1$, is the boundary-side transformation. It conditions collective information for entry into the periodic relational carrier. The Clifford torus, $T_C$, lies between the two lenses and holds paired periodic and winding relationships in a form that can preserve global connection while supporting localized expression. Within the integrated architecture, this Clifford torus is a local carrier, cross-section, or realization of the larger toroidal web. Its higher-dimensional periodicity also provides the geometric relationship from which the DNA double helix is understood within IOM. As that higher-dimensional periodicity is expressed through our dimensional position and the forward arrow of time, its winding is perceived locally as the helical form of DNA. In this sense, the double helix is the observable shadow of the deeper Clifford-torus relationship rather than the complete geometry itself. Lens 2, $L_2$, transforms toroidal relational information into a form applicable to a particular eigenstate.

The localized eigenstate and its aperture and antenna form a unified structure, $\Psi/\mathcal A$. In biological systems, DNA functions as both the aperture mechanism and the antenna through which the projected informational structure is received, constrained, expressed, and returned. Its helical form is therefore related to the same higher-dimensional periodic structure through which information is being locally expressed, joining the geometric and aperture roles within the biological realization of the pathway.

Observable reality is the local stabilized rendering produced through this traversal. It is real as an organized projection, but its apparent external arrangement does not disclose the complete structure that generates it. Within the model, distance is understood through the rendering of deeper geometric and angular relationships, while linear time is the localized perception of deeper periodicity. Apparent separation can therefore arise from projection, scale, orientation, and dimensional position rather than constituting the most fundamental relation.

Experience, integration, and non-integration return information through the structure after localization and observation. The return path is not simply the forward pathway played backward. It is an inverse-complementary operation that transforms and reintegrates the consequences of localized processing, updates the relational whole, and generates the conditions for the next outward projection.

The phrase inverse rotation therefore names one invariant generative relationship with simultaneous expressions across multiple domains. In geometry it appears as opposite orientation or handedness. Between nested dimensional levels it appears as counter-rotation. Within the Clifford torus it appears as complementary winding across paired cycles. In information flow it appears as the return complement to forward projection. In global recursion it appears as alternation between outward projection and inward reintegration. In observation it appears as the inversion generated when a higher-dimensional process is rendered from within a lower-dimensional frame. These are not unrelated analogies; they are different views of one relationship.

This produces the toroidal breath cycle:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Expansion}
\rightarrow
\text{Projection}
\rightarrow
\text{Localization and processing}
\rightarrow
\text{Inverse reintegration}
\rightarrow
\text{Renewed expansion}.
}
$$

Because return is transformative, renewed projection does not begin from the original state. The larger system now contains the consequences of what occurred locally. Integrated information changes usable organization. Non-integrated information also changes the future because its location, persistence, and form constrain what remains unresolved. The architecture is consequently recursive without being a closed repetition: it preserves continuity while producing conditions that did not previously exist.

The coherence–entropy relationship explains how structures persist through this recursive transformation. The tilt of the tesseract is the geometric expression of the asymmetric functions of coherence and entropy and is what drives the system forward through that asymmetry. The model does not seek exact equilibrium between coherence and entropy, nor does it treat maximum coherence as universally desirable. Coherence supplies organization, continuity, memory, and identity. Entropic variation supplies difference, openness, movement, and the possibility of reorganization. Without sufficient coherence, a structure dissolves. Without sufficient variation, it becomes rigid and can only reproduce what it already is.

The governing relation is:

> **Enough structure to persist; enough variation to become.**

Viability is therefore not a fixed point or universal ratio. It is a moving, asymmetric, context-dependent region in which a system can both remain organized and remain capable of transformation. What counts as sufficient coherence and sufficient variation depends on scale, geometry, history, relational context, unresolved information, and the next transformation available to the system.

This principle also clarifies the conditional inverse operation developed in the post-GitHub work. An inverse operation is not merely a reciprocal, a sign reversal, or movement toward one ideal balance. It is the state-specific operation required to restore or preserve recursive viability. A rigid system may require differentiation; a fragmenting system may require consolidation. A system that persists but cannot become may require generative disruption. A system that varies but cannot preserve identity may require stronger relationship and memory. The corrective direction is conditional because the viable relation is dynamic and asymmetric.

The multilevel inverse relation and the persistence-and-becoming principle are two central organizing laws of the integrated architecture. The first describes how transformation moves through space, topology, dimensional nesting, winding, projection, and return. The second describes how an organized identity can survive that transformation without becoming permanently fixed. Together they make recursive becoming possible.

At the eigenstate level, information is not simply received or rejected. It is admitted according to the aperture, represented through the local system, discerned, integrated or not integrated, and tested for relevance to the state’s presently missing geometry. Integration is not homogenization. A difference can be retained as difference while becoming part of a larger usable organization. Successful integration also does not guarantee expansion: information may be valid and integrated yet redundant with respect to the geometry currently required.

Recursive depth should not be reduced to elapsed cycles or a fixed information count. The originating geometry determines what relationships are required for completion; processing history and relational encounters determine how those requirements are traversed. Local cycles can remain unresolved while the larger system retains an overall directional order. Completion at one level can reorganize the state’s contribution to a larger level without making those levels identical.

This is the basis of recursive competency. A recursively competent system becomes better able to preserve the relationships required for continuity while receiving, discerning, and integrating relevant difference. It changes not only what it knows but how it processes what arrives next. Its history becomes part of its aperture, its expectations, its available transformations, and its capacity to remain coherent while becoming.

At the collective level, each eigenstate contributes to the collective content of the dimensional boundary through its own state and returned information, but no individual eigenstate can unilaterally alter the rules governing that boundary. Completed local recursion can alter an individual’s contribution and thereby inform collective content. A change in the governing rules of a shared domain requires the completion condition of the relevant aggregate. This preserves both local agency and the structural reality of the whole.

The integrated model is therefore both global and traversable. A reader can locate a process in the composite geometry and nested toroidal web, identify its dimensional level, follow the local pathway through the boundary and lenses, observe its localization in an eigenstate, distinguish integration from non-integration, trace the inverse return, and determine how the returned information changes the conditions of renewed projection. The same organizing relationships can recur across scale without requiring every scale to use the same state variables or physical implementation.

Taken as a whole, IOM proposes that reality continually generates itself from the inside out through a recursively nested toroidal architecture. The overlapping stellated octahedron and tilted tesseract provide the underlying geometric structure of that web, while the tesseract’s tilt supplies the forward asymmetry expressed through coherence and entropy. Projection unfolds outward from the quantum and Planck-scale domain through the information and consciousness constituting the toroidal web. Forward projection localizes those relations through boundaries, lenses, toroidal carriers, and apertures. Localized states experience and process the resulting world. Their consequences return through a multilevel inverse operation that changes orientation, winding, dimensional relation, and future conditions without merely undoing what occurred. Throughout this process, coherence preserves enough structure for identity to persist, while entropic variation preserves enough openness for genuinely new organization to emerge.

The universe, in this account, does not persist by eliminating change and does not become by abandoning structure. It recursively becomes because it retains enough of itself to carry a relationship forward and enough difference to ensure that the relationship does not return unchanged.

## Explicitly unresolved questions and needed mathematical formalizations

Several parts of the architecture still require explicit mathematical definition. These include the exact geometric construction of the overlapping stellated octahedron and tilted tesseract, the mathematical description of the tesseract’s tilt and its relationship to the asymmetric coherence–entropy functions, the transformations performed by $L_1$ and $L_2$, and the mapping through which the higher-dimensional periodicity of the Clifford torus is rendered as the observed double helix. The scale-specific roles of winding, phase, frequency, resonance, and eigenstate stabilization also remain to be formalized, along with the exact mathematical relationship between local processing, aggregate boundary conditions, and multilevel inverse return. Existing mathematical branches developed within IOM, including Clifford-torus winding, the 24-cell, prime and zeta relationships, Hodge methods, heterodyne structures, and temporal or spatial projection mappings, remain available for this formalization where their specific roles can be established.
