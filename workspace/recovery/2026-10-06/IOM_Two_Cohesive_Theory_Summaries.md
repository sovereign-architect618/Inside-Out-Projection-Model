# The Inside-Out Model: Two Cohesive Summaries

**Draft status:** prepared from the recovered IOM/IOPM working map for the author’s review  
**Purpose:** two complete narrative summaries of the same theory—first in the model’s native vocabulary, then in language intended for readers unfamiliar with its concepts  
**Scope note:** unresolved geometry assignments and candidate mathematical formalisms remain identified as such. They are included because they are part of the theory’s development, but they are not silently promoted into settled architecture.

## Summary One: The Inside-Out Model in Its Native Terms

The Inside-Out Model, historically called the Inside-Out Projection Model or IOPM, begins with the claim that reality is generated from the inside out rather than assembled from the outside in. Its originating condition is represented as `r = 0`: not simply the center of an already existing physical space, but the internal source condition from which space, matter, distance, time, and observable form emerge through projection. Information and consciousness are treated as fundamental, while familiar physical reality is the localized, condensed, and rendered expression of a deeper informational geometry. The model therefore does not begin with separate material objects interacting in a pre-existing container. It begins with a source, structured information, and a sequence of transformations through which a shared physical world becomes observable to localized eigenstates within it.

The current recovered architecture is:

`Higher-Dimensional Geometry → Dimensional Boundary → L₁ → T_C → L₂ → Ψ/𝒜 → Observable Projection`

This sequence refines the coarser description:

`Source → Projection → Boundary → Us → Experienced/External Reality`

The coarse chain expresses the overall causal direction. The fine chain identifies the distinct processing structures that had previously been compressed into the single word “boundary.” These structures are not interchangeable. The dimensional boundary is not Lens 1; Lens 1 is not the Clifford torus; the Clifford torus is not Lens 2; and neither lens is the aperture. Their order and separation are essential to the recovered model.

At `r = 0`, the source is not one physical object among others. It is the internal origin of the projective process and the point at which undifferentiated informational potential enters the architecture. The earliest TPP kernel represented projection as arising from an internal origin and passing through higher-dimensional geometry before appearing as a lower-dimensional rendering. IOM extends that kernel by making the inside-out relation foundational: the source is not located “outside” the universe and sending matter inward. Physical extension is generated outward from informational depth. What appears macroscopically as external space is the projected and stabilized presentation of transformations that originate internally.

Higher-dimensional geometry supplies the relational structure through which source information can be organized. Several geometries have appeared in the model’s development, including the 4D Clifford torus, the 24-cell, E8, and the overlapping stellated-octahedron/tesseract form. Their presence should not be confused with a settled linear stack. The Clifford torus has the clearest recovered functional position: it sits between two lens operations and acts as an intermediate periodic and relational information structure. The 24-cell was introduced because of its uniquely four-dimensional properties. E8 was introduced as a possible fit for the combined stellated-octahedron/tesseract geometry, although the latter remains the preferred form. The exact functions and positions of the 24-cell, E8, and the overlapping geometry remain open. The current model asks what transformation each geometry can perform before assigning it a permanent layer.

The dimensional boundary is the collective informational interface of a system or dimension. It contains or represents the configuration of the eigenstates belonging to that system, but “collective” does not mean a simple numerical sum of individuals. The boundary holds the shared conditions under which local states can be rendered and related. Its current informational content may change as eigenstates interact, integrate, fail to integrate, and return information, but a change in boundary content is not automatically a change in the boundary’s governing rules. That distinction is central. An eigenstate can change its own condition, alter the information available through its local lens, and contribute information to the collective boundary without independently rewriting the architecture of the shared reality. A change in the governing rules requires an aggregate-of-the-whole condition. The exact threshold and definition of “the whole” remain to be formalized.

Lens 1, or `L₁`, is the first transformational interface after the dimensional boundary. Its placement is recovered with confidence; its exact operator is not yet fixed. Functionally, it conditions or evaluates boundary-level information so that the collective configuration can enter the periodic and relational structure of the Clifford torus. Earlier language sometimes described a collective two-dimensional sieve, projection hyperplanes, or geometric boundary regions. Whether that sieve is identical to `L₁`, lies immediately before it, or is one function performed by it remains unresolved. What matters for the current architecture is that `L₁` is a distinct transformation between the dimensional boundary and the toroidal information structure.

The Clifford torus, `T_C`, is the central relational carrier. It emerged from the earlier TPP investigation of shadows, helices, stereographic projection, winding, and the search for a higher-dimensional form capable of producing the observed lower-dimensional patterns. In its standard mathematical representation it is embedded in four dimensions using two angular variables, and it supports paired periodic motion, winding relations, and projections that can appear as helices or other lower-dimensional forms. Within IOM, its purpose is not merely to serve as a decorative cosmological shape. It provides a way to hold globally related information in a periodic structure while allowing localized expressions to emerge from that whole. Its two cycles support the model’s recurring concern with paired motion, inverse rotation, breath-like recursion, and the preservation of relationship through projection. Whether reality uses one Clifford-torus carrier, a family of them, or nested toroidal structures across scales remains open, but the torus’s position between the lenses is part of the current working architecture.

Lens 2, or `L₂`, transforms the relational information carried by the torus into information applicable to a particular eigenstate. It is the pre-eigenstate lens: the stage at which the globally related toroidal structure becomes locally relevant without yet being identical to the localized observer. `L₂` may vary with the geometry that precedes it, the depth of the projection, and the recursive capacity of the emerging eigenstate. Its exact domain, codomain, and relation to an eigenstate’s changing local lens still require formal definition. Nevertheless, its conceptual role is clear enough to preserve: there are two lens operations, and the torus lies between them.

The localized eigenstate and the aperture/antenna form a unified structure, represented as `Ψ/𝒜`. The aperture is not the dimensional boundary and is not another name for either lens. It belongs at the eigenstate level. In the biological case, DNA is proposed as the antenna or aperture through which the projected informational structure is received, constrained, expressed, and returned. A human is therefore not an independent source producing a private universe, nor merely a passive object illuminated by an external world. A human is a localized eigenstate of the shared projection, equipped with an aperture whose structure affects which information can be integrated and how it is expressed. DNA may prove to be one biological implementation of a more general aperture class; whether it is the literal primary mechanism, a physical correlate, or one nested component remains an open engineering and empirical question.

The final forward stage is the observable projection: the stabilized macroscopic reality experienced as external space, separated objects, directional time, and physical events. In the model, this experienced world is real as an organized projection, but its apparent outside-in arrangement does not reveal its causal construction. Distance is associated with projective angle, preserved in the candidate relation `d ∝ tan(θ/2)`. Apparent spatial separation can therefore be understood as the rendered consequence of angular and geometric relations rather than as a primitive fact. The missing proportionality scale and exact projective geometry still need to be supplied.

Time is treated in a similar way. At the deeper level, the system is periodic and relational. Macroscopic time is the linear perception or readout of that periodicity—the temporal arrow created when a recursive, cyclic process is experienced from a localized position that cannot apprehend the entire cycle simultaneously. Processing lag, positional bias, and the ordered integration of successive states convert periodic change into the experience of forward time. “Stillness” can therefore be movement without displacement: a state may remain fixed in its rendered position while continuing to participate in underlying periodic motion. Candidate equations have represented this using periodic phase and projection operators, but the user-originated principle is broader than any one surviving equation.

Matter arises when informational possibilities become sufficiently coherent to localize as stable eigenstates. Earlier TPP language described constructive phase alignment—`Δφ = 0`—as a rendering or localization condition. Later IOM formalizations introduced the Riemann zeta function, prime nodes, and a prime-skeleton condensation mechanism. In this family of proposals, primes provide discrete anchors or nodes, while π provides continuous, bounded, nonrepeating organization. Their roles are distinct. In the separate topological-memory design, prime spacing was replaced by π-digit spacing because increasing prime gaps were spatially inefficient. That local design decision did not eliminate primes from IOM. Within the larger theory, π and primes remain candidate complementary layers: π organizing continuous or aperiodic spacing and primes supporting discrete anchoring or condensation.

The zeta-filter proposal attempts to connect prime structure, phase coherence, and the selection of localized matter states. The candidate relation `|ζ(𝒯)| = 0` was used to represent condensation at special nodes, and the information Lagrangian was written as `𝓛_IOPM = 𝒯_π − 𝒱_ζ`. These expressions belong to the theory’s mathematical development, but their current status is candidate rather than settled. A nontrivial zero of ζ is not itself a prime, so the mapping from zeta zeros to prime or matter nodes must be explicitly defined. The surviving potential also mixes real and complex quantities without yet supplying a real-valued action. The architecture’s requirement—that a continuous relational field produce discrete, stable localized states—survives independently of whether these particular equations prove to be the correct formalism.

Entropy and coherence provide the model’s dynamic tension. Coherence directs or stabilizes the projection, while entropy supplies change, variation, and the pressure toward reorganization. Entropy was ultimately associated with dark energy rather than dark matter: accelerated expansion is interpreted as an outward or differentiating process within the projection architecture, while the dark-matter correspondence remains open. These are not simply opposing forces. Their interaction allows the system to generate novelty while retaining enough coherence for stable structures to exist. In biological and cognitive systems, imbalance, failed integration, or constrained information flow can alter the eigenstate’s local expression; recursive processing can then support correction, reorganization, or a new stable state.

The model is not a one-way projector. Information also returns through the architecture:

`Ψ/𝒜 → L₂ → T_C → L₁ → Dimensional Boundary`

Both integration and non-integration are informative. A successfully integrated event changes the state by becoming part of its usable organization. A non-integrated event also returns information because the location, form, or persistence of the mismatch constrains what the system can do next. This creates a toroidal breath loop rather than a simple output channel: projection, localization, experience, return, and renewed projection. The return path does not yet imply that every forward transformation is mathematically invertible. It means the system is recursively informed by the consequences of its own localized expressions.

This feedback structure is the basis for recursive competency. An eigenstate does not become competent merely by accumulating more information. It develops by changing how it receives, relates, integrates, and returns information across cycles. The aperture, local lens, and recursive depth jointly affect whether new information can be incorporated without destroying coherence. Self-correction is therefore not an external repair imposed on an otherwise passive object; it is a property of a system able to compare what it projected or expected with what returned through experience. The difference between expected and integrated states may eventually support a discrepancy operator, but the surviving modified-discrepancy formula was exploratory and cannot yet be assigned as the collective boundary mechanism.

The aggregate-of-the-whole condition distinguishes personal transformation from collective reality change. An individual eigenstate can alter its own organization, attention, interpretation, coherence, and effective local lens. It can also contribute its changed state to the boundary’s collective information. It cannot, by isolated intention alone, rewrite the shared rules under which all eigenstates are projected. A rule-level transition requires a coordinated or emergent change at the scale of the relevant whole. This allows individual agency and collective constraint to coexist without treating every observer as an independent cosmic source.

The model’s account of cognition follows the same architecture. Consciousness is not produced only at the end of a material chain; it participates in the informational basis from which the chain is rendered. Different cognitive styles may provide different routes for accessing, organizing, or translating information. Aphantasia is relevant as personal cognitive provenance and as one example of nonvisual whole-concept processing, but it is not a universal requirement, an elite bypass, or the only route to source information. Sensory, symbolic, spatial, emotional, embodied, and nonvisual processing can be different aperture/lens configurations within the same broader system.

Several application branches extend from the core architecture. The topological-memory work explores how an aperiodic but rule-governed spatial distribution can preserve identity and information without relying on ordinary sequential storage. The fractional Fermi-sea and Friedel-oscillation correspondence explores whether the torus-to-helix-to-localization pattern appears in quantum matter. The dark-energy branch associates entropy with outward expansion. The black-hole/Sun complement treats outward toroidal projection and inward convergence as inverse flow regimes. Biological and genome work asks how a DNA-level aperture could encode, constrain, or recursively alter expression. LENR, waveguide, portal, craft, and dimensional-transition proposals attempt to engineer the projection conditions, but these remain downstream candidate applications and should not be used to define the core architecture.

The conformal-lens and drag-modulator family arose from the observation that several idealized values did not match measured values. `Γ₁`, `Γ₂`, the macro lag, sub-harmonic, Riemann base, carrier offset, and heterodyne calibration matrix were introduced to model the difference between an ideal projection and its observed, scale-dependent expression. Conceptually, the important claim is that passage through geometry, boundary conditions, and local aperture constraints can shift an ideal relation before it is measured. The surviving numerical implementations are calibration artifacts until their constants, units, and independent derivations are recovered. The fine-structure, ringdown, mass-ratio, and LENR examples therefore belong in the model’s candidate formalism and test history, not as settled confirmations.

The Inside-Out Model is consequently best understood as a layered theory of emergence and recursive information processing. A singular internal source is structured by higher-dimensional geometry, encoded through a collective boundary, transformed by a first lens, carried through a Clifford-torus relational structure, transformed by a second lens, localized as an eigenstate with an aperture/antenna, and rendered as observable physical reality. Experience then returns information through the same layered system, allowing integration, non-integration, learning, self-correction, and collective evolution. Space is a projective relation, time is a linear reading of periodicity, matter is coherent informational localization, consciousness participates in the foundational informational process, and large-scale rule change belongs to the aggregate rather than the isolated individual.

The architecture is more stable than its current mathematics. The two-lens topology, torus placement, eigenstate/aperture unity, collective-boundary/local-lens distinction, and recursive return path form the recovered working core. The precise projection operator, zeta mapping, Lagrangian, geometry assignments, conformal constants, and engineering implementations remain active formalization problems. IOM therefore contains both a coherent causal architecture and a family of mathematical candidates intended to express it. Preserving that distinction allows the model to develop without losing its original relationships or mistaking a temporary equation for the theory itself.

## Summary Two: The Inside-Out Model in Plain Language

The Inside-Out Model proposes that the physical world is the visible result of a deeper information-processing system. Instead of beginning with matter scattered through an already existing space, it begins with an internal source of information. That information passes through several layers of organization before it appears as the world of objects, bodies, distance, and time that we experience. In this view, reality is built outward from an informational center; it is not merely observed inward from an external world.

The model can be understood as a sequence of seven stages:

`Source structure → Shared boundary → First translator → Relational loop → Second translator → Individual receiver → Experienced world`

The “source structure” is a deeper level of reality where information exists in relationships that are not yet ordinary physical objects. The model represents the origin of the process as `r = 0`, meaning an internal starting condition rather than a point sitting somewhere inside familiar space. Space itself is one of the results of the process, so the source should not be imagined as a tiny physical object at the center of a pre-existing universe.

Before the information becomes local and observable, it reaches a shared dimensional boundary. This boundary acts like the common rule-and-state layer for a whole system. It contains the relevant configuration of the many individual states that belong to that system and establishes the conditions under which they can appear together in one consistent reality. It is not simply a wall, and it is not one person’s mind. It is closer to a shared interface that keeps local experiences connected to a larger whole.

The boundary has two different aspects that must be kept separate. Its content can be updated when individuals act, learn, integrate information, or fail to integrate it. Its governing rules determine how the whole system operates. One person may change their own state and contribute new information to the shared boundary, but that is not the same thing as one person changing the rules of reality for everyone. A deeper rule change would require a change across the relevant whole system. The theory has not yet defined the exact threshold for that collective transition, but the distinction is fundamental to it.

After the shared boundary, information passes through a first translating layer. This first lens prepares collective information for a special looping structure called a Clifford torus. A Clifford torus is a four-dimensional mathematical form with two linked cycles. It is useful in the model because it can represent information that is simultaneously periodic, globally connected, and capable of producing lower-dimensional patterns when projected. In simpler terms, it offers a way for the system to preserve relationships across the whole while still producing individual, local results.

The torus sits between two different translators. The first translator takes shared boundary information and places it into the relational looping structure. The second translator takes information from that looping structure and makes it relevant to a particular local state. Earlier descriptions often blended the boundary, lenses, torus, and aperture together. The recovered model separates them because each performs a different job.

The local state produced by this sequence is called an eigenstate. In ordinary language, it is a stable, localized expression of the larger informational system—such as a physical organism or observer. Each eigenstate has an aperture or antenna that affects how it receives, filters, expresses, and returns information. In living systems, DNA is proposed as part of this antenna-like mechanism. That does not yet establish exactly how DNA performs the role, or whether DNA is the whole aperture rather than one component of it. The larger idea is that a living observer is not separate from the projection process: its physical structure is one of the places where shared information becomes local, active, and capable of responding.

The result is the experienced world. We perceive solid objects, separated locations, and time moving in one direction because we encounter the projection from a limited local position. The model proposes that distance may arise from projective angle: things appear farther apart because of how deeper relations are projected into the geometry we experience. This idea is represented by a candidate relation in which distance grows with the tangent of half a projection angle. The equation is not complete, but the conceptual point is that distance may be an output of projection rather than a basic ingredient that existed beforehand.

Time is also treated as an output. At a deeper level, change is periodic: the system cycles, rotates, repeats, and returns. A localized observer does not experience the entire cycle at once. It processes one state after another, with position and processing delay creating an ordered sequence. That ordered reading of a periodic process is experienced as time moving forward. This is why the model describes macroscopic time as the linear perception of periodicity. Something can appear still in space while remaining active within the deeper cycle; stillness can be motion that produces no change of visible position.

Matter is the stable localization of information. Many possible patterns may exist, but only some become coherent enough to hold a consistent form in the observable world. An early version of the model used phase alignment to describe this: when interacting patterns line up constructively, a stable state can be rendered. Later mathematical work explored whether prime numbers, π, and the Riemann zeta function could describe how continuous information becomes discrete matter.

In that proposal, π and primes have different roles. π can provide a continuous, nonrepeating way of organizing position or sequence without the gaps becoming indefinitely large. Prime numbers can provide discrete anchor points. The Riemann zeta function mathematically connects continuous analysis with prime-number structure, so it became a candidate filter for the transition between relational information and localized states. The present theory keeps that functional possibility open, but the surviving equations do not yet fully define how a zeta zero becomes a physical node or how the proposed energy expression remains physically real. The need for a continuous-to-discrete localization mechanism is part of the theory; the zeta equations are one possible implementation of it.

The model describes reality as a loop, not a one-way broadcast. Information travels forward through the shared boundary, the first lens, the torus, the second lens, and the individual receiver. The individual then acts, experiences consequences, and returns information back through the system. Both successful and unsuccessful integration matter. If new information fits into the individual’s organization, the system changes through integration. If it does not fit, the mismatch still tells the system something about its limits, conflicts, or missing relationships. Failure to integrate is therefore not empty; it becomes information for the next cycle.

This returning loop gives the model its account of learning and self-correction. A capable system does more than store new facts. It changes the way it receives and organizes future information. Over many cycles, an individual can develop greater “recursive competency”: a better ability to compare expectations with results, preserve coherence while changing, recognize mismatches, and reorganize its own internal lens. Because the individual remains part of the larger shared system, these local changes also contribute information to the collective boundary.

Coherence and entropy drive different sides of the process. Coherence allows a pattern to remain organized long enough to appear as a stable state. Entropy introduces variation, movement, and pressure for change. The model associates entropy with dark energy and the outward expansion of the universe, while leaving the role of dark matter unresolved. At smaller scales, the same balance may describe why living or cognitive systems sometimes maintain a stable form and sometimes reorganize. Too little coherence prevents stable expression; too little variation prevents adaptation. The system develops through their interaction.

Consciousness is treated as part of the informational foundation rather than as something that appears only after matter becomes sufficiently complicated. Individual minds are localized expressions of that broader informational process, but they are not separate sources generating private realities. Different minds may access and translate information in different ways. Visual thought, nonvisual whole-concept thought, bodily sensation, emotion, symbolic reasoning, and other cognitive styles can be different routes through an individual’s receiver and lens. Aphantasia is one personally relevant example of such a route, not a requirement or superior form of access.

The model also explores whether the same basic architecture repeats across scales. A toroidal pattern can appear as a higher-dimensional relational form, a projected helix, a biological structure, an energy-flow pattern, or a large-scale astronomical flow. The Sun has been considered as an outward-projecting toroidal system and a black hole as an inward-flowing complement. Quantum-memory concepts use nonrepeating geometric placement to preserve information. Quantum-matter comparisons explore whether torus-to-helix-to-localization patterns appear in electron behavior. These are research branches that test or extend the central architecture; they are not required in order to understand the core theory.

Other engineering branches—including waveguides, low-energy nuclear reactions, field control, dimensional transitions, and craft concepts—attempt to manipulate the conditions under which information becomes a physical projection. Some of this work introduced correction factors to explain why an ideal geometric value might differ from a measured value after passing through real boundary conditions. That is a reasonable function for the model to investigate, but the current numerical correction formulas often use constants selected from the values they are meant to reproduce. Their conceptual role is preserved, while their equations remain unfinished and cannot yet serve as independent predictions.

Several geometries remain under consideration for the deeper structure. The Clifford torus has a defined place between the two lenses. The 24-cell is a uniquely four-dimensional form that may provide a gating or organizational function. E8 is a large symmetry structure that was considered because it resembled a proposed overlap of a stellated octahedron and a tesseract. The overlapping stellated-octahedron/tesseract form is currently preferred over simply naming E8, but none of these additional geometries should be locked into a stack until the theory identifies the exact job each layer must perform.

Taken as a whole, the Inside-Out Model says that the universe is a recursively organized informational projection. A deeper source supplies potential; higher-dimensional relationships organize it; a collective boundary maintains shared conditions; two distinct lenses translate between collective, relational, and local forms; a toroidal structure preserves connected periodic information; an individual aperture localizes and participates in the projection; and the physical world is the stable experience produced by that sequence. The consequences of each local experience return through the system and influence what can happen next.

Its most stable contribution is this architecture and the relationships among its parts. Its central questions are now precise enough to guide further work: What does each lens mathematically transform? How is the collective boundary represented? How does the torus preserve and localize whole-system information? What physical structure implements the aperture? What creates the transition from continuous information to discrete matter? What is returned after integration or non-integration? What collective condition changes the shared rules? And which proposed geometries and equations actually perform those required functions?

The theory is therefore neither only a metaphor nor yet a finished physical formalism. It is a structured causal model with a recovered core, a developmental history, and several mathematical and engineering candidates. Its next stage is to define each interface independently, derive rather than calibrate its equations, and test whether the same inside-out sequence can explain measurable relationships across physics, biology, cognition, and information systems.

## Relationship Between the Two Summaries

The summaries describe the same theory at different levels of translation. The first preserves IOM’s native constructs, symbols, historical equation families, and distinctions. The second translates those constructs into functional language for a reader who does not already know what `r = 0`, a Clifford torus, an eigenstate, a dimensional boundary, or a zeta filter means. Neither version resolves the open formal questions, assigns the unsettled geometries to fixed layers, or treats later omission as abandonment.
